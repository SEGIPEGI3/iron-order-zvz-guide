ZvZ Field Guide

A simple, picture-based guide to big group fights in Albion Online — from your first CTA to reviewing your own VODs. Easy words, no translator needed. This guide teaches the reason behind each rule, not one fixed comp, so it still works when the meta changes.

01 · Getting Started

This part is for new guild members. It helps you become useful in ZvZ fast, without learning every advanced idea at once.

First mindset Your caller gives direction. But you still play your own character. Your job: stay close to the group, know your role, don't stack with other players, listen to calls but keep thinking for yourself, use your defensive skills before you die, and review mistakes without blaming only the enemy.
Ten rules before every CTA
1
Don't stack together.Groups that stack die fast to enemy AoE damage.
2
Don't run too far.Run far enough to survive and turn back. Don't leave the fight.
3
If enemy cooldowns are used, look to attack.Running after you survive their damage gives them free time.
4
Direction calls have a real purpose."South" can mean an angle, a path, a fake move, or a turn setup.
5
A healthy tank must not hide.A tank that backs into the DPS line makes things worse, not safer.
6
DPS damage must land on time.Starting your cast at zero is usually too late.
7
Support skills must land early.Late CC, late anti-heal, or late shields often do nothing.
8
Healers must heal the danger zone.Safe but useless healing is still bad healing.
9
Open the map. Read the terrain.Walls, chokepoints, doors, and corners decide fights.
10
Review what you did, not just the result.Winning doesn't mean you played well. Losing doesn't mean every call was wrong.

How to practice: pick one rule per CTA. Don't try to fix all ten at once.

02 · Fight Basics

Learn the shared language of ZvZ before you focus on one role. If you know these basics, you can watch any fight and ask the right questions: what style is each side playing? Who controls the distance and the angle? Which cooldowns matter right now?

BASICS · 01

Fight styles

Brawl, bomb, kite, control, and ranged pressure are different styles to recognize — not a ranking of "best" to "worst."

BASICS · 02

Fight flow

Every fight has stages: contact, engage, counter, reset, chase, and pressure on the objective.

BASICS · 03

Timing and tempo

Cooldown windows and the rhythm of engages. Tempo is a group skill, not just one player's job.

BASICS · 04

Pressure

Damage is not the only type of pressure. Position, timing, information, and disruption can win fights too.

BASICS · 05

Spacing and AoE

Good spacing lowers the value of enemy AoE damage. Spreading out does not mean running away.

The basics rule Don't only ask "what weapon is good?" Ask: "what problem does this role, call, or tool solve on the battlefield?" This question keeps you flexible when the patch, the meta, or your roster changes.

03 · In the Fight

This is what happens between "I understand the idea" and "I did the right thing while the screen was exploding." Simple rules that work in almost any comp.

FIGHT · 01

Damage lands at zero

Your hit should land exactly when the countdown ends — not start after it ends.

FIGHT · 02

Spread is not leaving

Spreading out lowers enemy AoE damage. You still stay close enough to do your job.

FIGHT · 03

Q/W pressure after the bomb

An engage creates a short window. Q/W pressure is how you turn that window into real damage.

FIGHT · 04

Enemy cooldowns are used

If the enemy already used their key cooldowns, red names near you can be a target, not a threat.

FIGHT · 05

Survive the first hit

Many comps win just by surviving the first enemy hit without stacking together.

04 · Guild Roles

A role is not just a weapon. A role is a job in the fight. The same weapon can be played badly if you don't understand what problem your role should solve.

"Know what your role protects, what it pressures, what it stops, and what it enables."

Every role should be able to answer four questions: Where should I stand so the next call can work? What danger or chance am I responsible for? What value do I add between big cooldowns? Did my movement make the group safer, or more clumped?

Role families
ROLE

All players

Basic habits for everyone: stay close to the group, understand calls, spread out but stay in the fight, and learn from repeated mistakes.

ROLE

DPS

Turn short windows into kills. Focus on timing, angles, target choice, and Q/W uptime.

ROLE

Healers

ROLE

Support

Own the danger area with defensive tools, good timing, and help beyond just damage.

ROLE

Defensive / stopper tanks

Create safety. Block enemy paths, cover exits, stop counter-attacks, and stay until your team is safe.

ROLE

Engage / clump tanks

Group enemies together before the damage lands, so your team can kill them fast.

ROLE

Callers / officers

Turn information, terrain, cooldowns, and enemy style into clear, useful calls.

ROLE

Scouts / info

Give useful information without spamming comms or taking over the main caller's job.

What each role does, phase by phase
PhaseDPSHealersSupportTanksCallers
Before contact Stay close to the group. Find good range. Don't clump too early. Stand where danger will likely be, not the safest spot. Read the enemy's mood. Get danger tools ready. Set up front and side cover. Decide the terrain goal and the likely enemy style.
Your team engages Damage lands on the window, not after it. Heal the front line and players coming back from danger. Help the push, but expect a counter-attack. Create or hold a good kill chance. Give the next step: hold, push, reset, or switch target.
Enemy engages Spread out, survive, then look for a Q/W chance or a counter. Heal the hit and the players at the back. Cleanse, shield, or block real danger. Stop the enemy's damage path and follow up. Tell if it's a bait or a full engage. Check enemy cooldowns.
Post-bomb window Use Q/W pressure. Finish low-HP targets. Don't run away automatically. Keep the pressure players alive. Protect the push and the exit path. Stop the enemy from resetting or counter-attacking. Decide if enemy cooldowns are spent enough to push in.
Crossing / objective Once safe, turn and help the next wave. Heal the danger zone, not just the first players through. Use tools where the next wave will cross. Be the last one out, or hold the line. Call the route, the side, and the exit clearly.
Role review checklist (VOD)
  • Was I close enough to do my job?
  • Did I use my tools on real danger or a real chance?
  • Did I help the players who make the call work?
  • Did I add value between the big cooldowns?
  • Did I create clumps, or help reduce them?
  • Did I understand the meaning of the call, or just follow the words?
  • Did I move because of real danger, or because red names made me nervous?
  • Did I save my cooldown for the right moment, or waste it too early?

05 · Calls & Comms

ZvZ is not only about mechanics. It's about good information. A caller decides based on terrain, enemy movement, cooldowns, the kill feed, scouts, the minimap, and your roster. But you also need to think for yourself — don't just follow words blindly.

COMMS · 01

Calls have a purpose

A direction call is not just "walk this way." It's an instruction to create a certain situation on the field.

COMMS · 02

Threat + Location + Action

A good callout tells the group what the danger is, where it is, and what to do about it.

COMMS · 03

Clean information

Reports should help decisions. Don't spam comms or exaggerate just to boost morale.

COMMS · 04

Caller decision-making

How a caller weighs the comp, terrain, cooldowns, and the next move.

COMMS · 05

Still think for yourself

The caller is not your eyes, your hands, or your brain. Keep reading the fight yourself.

Core rule Better information means better calls, better role decisions, and a cleaner VOD review. Bad information makes good players late, and makes callers guess.

06 · Terrain & Objectives

Terrain decides which calls are even possible. Objectives decide what movement costs you. A fight at a castle, territory, wall, gate, or bridge is still a normal ZvZ fight. Spacing, timing, support, healing, and pressure still matter.

TERRAIN · 01

Walls, chokepoints, bridges, and corners shape what a caller can safely ask for.

TERRAIN · 02

Chokepoints, bridges, gates

TERRAIN · 03

Angle control

Which side you hold, and how much force you put there, can win or lose the fight before it even starts.

TERRAIN · 04

Castles & territories

Objectives change the shape of the fight and the risk. But basic discipline still applies.

TERRAIN · 05

One angle at a time

If you don't need to fight on many sides, don't let the enemy attack you from many sides.

07 · Training & VOD Review

Getting better takes more than reading rules. Players need clear questions, drills, repetition, and an honest way to separate "we won" from "we played well."

"A mistake is only useful if it turns into a clearer next action."

Don't review a fight only to find someone to blame. Review it to find the next drill, the next role fix, or the next calling improvement.
